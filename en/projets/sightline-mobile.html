Personal project, mobile app (installable web app and Android APK), designed as a UI/UX portfolio piece

Sightline mobile: a CS2 player's match notebook, in the pocket

Sightline is used on a computer. But a player needs their information at other moments: before the match to prepare for the opponent, right after for the review, and during practice, with grenades in view next to the keyboard. I designed a mobile app for those moments, with the same accounts and data as the site. I had it built by AI agents in two days, from specification to release.

Four moments in a player's week

I started from the moments when a player takes out their phone, not from the site's features. So the app does not copy all of Sightline: it answers four precise needs.

Demo-mode screenshots at iPhone size: public professional matches (Team Vitality against MOUZ), dates recalculated to the day the app is opened. The interface is in French.

Interface choices, and why

  • The home screen knows what moment it is. The next match comes first, with a countdown; a review under 24 hours old that has not been opened goes before everything. This rule is isolated and tested.
  • In the prep, every figure shows its reliability (anecdotal, indicative or solid) and its sample size. A deliberate design choice: never present two rounds as a trend.
  • Grenades are made for a phone lying next to the keyboard: the screen stays on, angles are shown large, and the practice command is copied in one gesture (with a light vibration in the native build).
  • Navigation designed as native: system tab bar and swipe back in the native build (written, not yet tested on a device); large titles that collapse on scroll and a tab bar with the same design in the web version.
  • A visual system of its own, “Carnet tactique”: dark background, a single accent colour, blue and amber reserved for the two game sides; the system font for text (in the native build it follows the size set on the phone), Saira for titles, fixed-width figures so they line up; a 4-point grid and 44-point touch targets.
  • Restrained motion: transitions of 100 to 220 ms and damped springs, replaced by simple fades when the user asks for reduced motion; a lineup sheet follows the finger and closes according to the gesture's momentum.
  • Accessibility: screen-reader labels, and a one-sentence summary for every chart: the progress curve is read out as a sentence saying whether the rating is going up or down.
  • A demo without an account, with embedded data from public pro matches rather than a shared account: a public account would have exposed, and let people change, the team's data.
  • A fifth tab, Carnet (notebook) (21 and 22 October): the team's strategy book (positions, grenades, plan), read-only since creating stays on PC so as not to overload a small screen, and notes. A note is written on a map, can be attached to a match, an opponent or a strategy, and stays private or is shared with the team; a teammate's note can be read but not edited. Today shows how many notes to re-read before the next opponent, and the site reads and writes notes too.

Free, without an app store

Starting constraint: zero cost. Publishing on the App Store costs €99 a year, and Google Play a one-off $25. So the app ships as an installable web app (added to the home screen from the browser) and as an Android APK built for free in the cloud. Notifications go through the web, since native notifications need the paid Apple account on iPhone (and Firebase on Android). When the stores become useful, the code will not need to change.

Under the hood

  • The same accounts as the site. The server now accepts token sign-in for the native app; on the web, the app keeps the site's secure session cookie and stores no token in the browser.
  • Notifications when a match is analysed or a prep report is ready. The server only accepts known notification services, so it cannot be made to call any address.
  • A blank screen on iPhone, found in a real test and fixed. Under Safari, loading several fonts at once got stuck and the app never appeared. Each font now goes through the FontFace API, with no shared stylesheet, and the app no longer waits more than 4 seconds for its fonts: if one fails, it shows with the fallback font.
  • Full screen on iPhone once installed, respecting the notch and the home bar (checked in a browser simulating an iPhone).
  • A light demo: about 400 KB of embedded data, under the 1.5 MB target set in the specification.

Known limits

  • The native version has never run on a real phone. Only the installable web version was tested, in simulated browsers and on my iPhone.
  • No offline mode: the app updates with every deployment and its service worker caches nothing.
  • A few targets under 44 points: filters are 36 points (enlarged touch area in the native build only) and segmented controls 40 points.
  • French and dark theme only; in the Carnet tab, strategies can be viewed but not edited, only notes can be written.
